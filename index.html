Social Media Strategy

On social media, you're a guest on someone else's platform, and the platform decides who sees your posts. A good strategy starts from that fact instead of fighting it.

Objective 1Describe the parts of a social media campaign plan and use it to design a campaign.
Objective 2Choose the best platform(s) for different social media goals.
Objective 3Pick the measures that show whether a campaign worked.

"Doing social media" is more than posting

Most people think social media marketing means posting on a company profile. Posting is only one of eight activities, and usually not the one that makes the most money.

Owned

Posting content

Free to do, but platforms show brand posts to few people.

Goal

Creating new customers

Usually done best with ads, not posts.

Goal

Increasing loyalty

Keeping past customers coming back. Posting works better here.

Indirect

SEO

Shared content earns links, which help search rankings a little.

Strongest

Paid advertising

Targeted ads are the most reliable way to find new customers on social.

Defense

Reputation management

Protecting how the brand is seen when things go wrong.

Close-up

Social monitoring

Reading specific comments about the brand and replying to them.

Big picture

Social listening

Summarizing the whole conversation about a brand or topic.

Watch out: social listening is cheap research, but people who post about a brand aren't a typical sample of its customers. Angry and very happy customers post; most customers say nothing.

Why posting is harder than it looks

Three problems limit how much a company's posts can do. The third is the one most people miss.

Problem 1

People don't follow brands

Users come to social media to be entertained, not sold to.

Problem 2

Platforms limit brand reach

Platforms would rather you pay for reach, so free brand posts get shown to few followers.

Problem 3

Seeing isn't buying

Even when people see your post, it may not change what they buy. Ask what the post teaches or offers.

What's changed: feeds on Facebook, Instagram, TikTok and YouTube now fill up with posts from accounts people don't follow, picked by an algorithm. Follower count matters less than it used to, and one post that people engage with can travel far beyond your followers. Most brand posts still reach very few people for free.

Paid, owned and earned media

Paid: you buy the exposure. Owned: you control the channel. Earned: other people give you the exposure. Sort each item below.

0 / 0
All paid

Reliable, but costs rise as easy customers run out, and it's an expensive way to keep the customers you already have.

All owned

No way to find anyone new.

All earned

Fragile. Upworthy built a huge site on viral sharing, and lost most of its traffic when Facebook changed its algorithm.

Earned media is a lottery ticket. Most efforts return little, but a few return 100 times what they cost. It's worth some effort, but it can't be the foundation.

Choosing a platform

Count potential customers, not total users. A huge platform full of people who will never buy from you is worth less than a small one full of buyers.

Six questions to ask

About the platform

  • Users: are my potential customers here?
  • Interaction: do people slow down, or scroll fast?
  • Ad tools: can I target and measure well?

About the ads

  • Attention: will the ad actually be noticed?
  • Targeting: can I skip people who will never buy?
  • Cost: is it cheap enough to pay off?

Facebook & Instagram

Meta's ad system is the default starting point for most consumer brands: huge reach and strong tools.

TikTok

Short vertical video and a feed driven almost entirely by recommendations. Strong for audiences under 35.

YouTube

The most-used platform. Skippable ads are guaranteed only 5 seconds, so the opening has to grab people.

LinkedIn

The first choice for B2B. Ads are expensive, so they need to convert better to pay off.

Pinterest

People come here to plan purchases and projects, which suits home, food, fashion and parenting brands.

X & Snapchat

X users scroll fast past ads; Snapchat skews young and needs content made for it.

What's changed: since Apple's 2021 privacy change limited tracking, Meta has cut many detailed targeting options and pushes automated "Advantage+" campaigns that pick the audience for you. The ad itself now does much of the targeting: an ad about screen-free afternoons finds a different parent than an ad about better grades.

Planning content

Nobody follows a brand unless it gives them something. Decide what value you're offering, in what format, and where the content comes from.

Measuring success

Judge each post against its purpose. A post meant to build goodwill shouldn't be graded on sales, and a sales post shouldn't be graded on likes.

Volume

How many saw it

Followers, impressions. Follower count is a vanity metric: it looks good and rarely tracks profit.

Engagement

Did they interact

Likes, comments, shares, saves, engagement rate. Shares and saves mean the most.

Conversion

Did they act

Click-through rate, conversion rate, share of followers who bought.

Engagement rate calculator

Engagement rate = (likes + comments + shares + saves) ÷ reach

Older textbooks divide likes by followers. Because feeds now show posts to many non-followers, most tools divide all engagements by reach or impressions.

What makes content go viral

Every person who watches creates X more views. If X is above 1, views keep growing. If it's below 1, the content fades out.

X = pf × N × pv
Each viewer creates
1.50 views

Views per round, starting from 1,000 viewers:

Raise pf

Surprise, humor, timeliness, controversy, a share button. This is the one that matters most.

Raise N

You rarely control it, except by paying to promote the post.

Raise pv

A strong thumbnail and a clear title.

The formula's blind spot: friend groups overlap. When you share a video, many of your friends have already seen it. Content can saturate one group (your campus, say) and never get out.

Viral isn't the same as persuasive

Catherine Tucker (MIT) showed 24,367 people one of 396 viral video ads, or an unrelated video as a control, then asked how likely they were to buy the brand.

Finding 1

Seeing the ad helped

People who watched an ad were more likely to say they'd buy than people who watched the control video.

Finding 2

More views, less persuasion

The more viral the ad, the less it raised purchase intent. To get shared, ads went funny or outrageous and dropped the sales message.

Still persuasive

Humor and strong visuals got shared without losing much persuasive power.

Less persuasive

Outrageous content got shared but persuaded less.

Tucker, C. (2015). The reach and persuasiveness of viral video ads. Marketing Science.

Word of mouth beats almost everything

Researchers tracked sign-ups at a social networking site after three kinds of boosts: a friend's referral, a mention in the press, and a marketing event.

Referral (word of mouth)Press coverageMarketing event

Approximate curves redrawn from Trusov, Bucklin & Pauwels (2009), Journal of Marketing.

Bigger

A referral produced the largest jump in sign-ups.

Longer

The referral effect lasted about three weeks. Press and events faded within about a week.

Why it matters: over the long run, referrals were roughly 20–30 times as effective as events or press mentions. That's why so many companies pay customers to refer friends.

Key terms

Organic content
Posts a company publishes for free on its own profiles.
Paid media
Exposure you buy: social, search and display ads, affiliates.
Owned media
Channels you control: website, social profiles, email list.
Earned media
Exposure others give you: shares, press, word of mouth, SEO.
Social monitoring
Reading and replying to specific posts about the brand.
Social listening
Summarizing the overall conversation about a brand or topic.
Style guide
Rules for tone, responses and content so many posters sound like one brand.
User-generated content
Photos, videos and reviews made by customers rather than the brand.
Curated content
Good content from others that the brand shares.
Vanity metric
A number that looks impressive but doesn't track profit, like follower count.
Engagement rate
Total interactions divided by reach or impressions.
Sentiment
The share of posts about a brand that are positive, neutral or negative.
Virality (X)
New views each viewer creates: pf × N × pv. Above 1 means it spreads.
Word of mouth (WOM)
People recommending a product to each other, in person or online.
